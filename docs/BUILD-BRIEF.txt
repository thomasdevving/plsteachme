# Build: "plsteach.me" — landing / demo website (waitlist + validation)

## 1. Your role and the goal

You are a senior front-end engineer and conversion-focused product designer. Build a fast, beautiful, honest landing website for **plsteach.me**, an AI visual coach that teaches practical, physical skills by watching what you do through your phone camera and guiding you step by step.

The website has four jobs, in this order:
1. Make visitors instantly understand the problem and the product (within 5 seconds of landing).
2. Collect waitlist signups **plus validation data** (which skills people want, what they'd pay, where they came from).
3. Work as the destination for social media traffic (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts): fast on mobile, great link previews, campaign tracking.
4. Capture interest from organizations (schools, youth work, vocational education, employers) through a separate short form.

Work through all phases (section 13) without stopping for confirmation. Fix errors autonomously. Keep it simple.

## 2. Product summary (source of truth for copy)

- Name: plsteach.me (confirmed name; keep it in one config constant)
- Mission: "Nobody should have to figure everything out alone."
- One-liner: "An AI coach that watches you practice real-life skills — and helps you get them right."
- The problem: Most people learn practical skills from a parent, family member or mentor. Not everyone had that person: some grew up without a parent figure, others had parents without the time or knowledge. Tutorials show how something is done, but can't see how YOU do it. A video will never tell you you're holding it wrong.
- The solution: Pick a skill, point your phone camera at yourself, and follow along. plsteach.me watches each step, tells you when you've got it, and gently corrects you when something's off — like a patient instructor standing next to you.
- How it works (the loop): Camera → recognizes what you're doing → checks your technique → gives feedback → checks again.
- Status: early demo / beta. First skill: tying a tie. Coming next: shaving, trimming a beard, ironing a shirt, sewing on a button, basic tool use (wording can be refined).
- Positioning principle: plsteach.me does not replace a parent or mentor. It makes the practical know-how and patient guidance you'd normally get from one available to everyone. The site must say this clearly and warmly.

## 3. Honesty rules (non-negotiable)

- Do not claim accuracy numbers, user counts, testimonials, partnerships, press logos, or awards that don't exist. Use no fake social proof.
- Label the product clearly as an early demo / beta.
- Describe privacy accurately: camera processing happens on the device; a few still frames per step are sent to an AI service to check the step and are not stored. Do not promise anything stronger.
- No medical, skincare or safety claims.

## 4. Audience

- Primary: people roughly 16–30 who never really learned everyday practical skills and feel a bit awkward about it. Many arrive from short-form video on their phone.
- Secondary: people who support others (older siblings, mentors, youth workers, foster families).
- Organizations: schools and vocational education (mbo), youth work, social organizations, employers who onboard young people.

## 5. Language

- Dutch copy: natural, informal ("je"), warm, not a literal translation of the English. Short sentences. Avoid anglicisms where natural Dutch exists, but "AI" and "app" are fine.
- Provide both language versions of all copy. I will review the copy, so keep it in clearly organized message files.

## 8. Waitlist form and validation data

Fields:
- Email (required)
- "Which skills would you like to learn?" (multi-select chips; options from the skills list plus "Something else" with a free-text field)
- Age bracket (required): under 16 / 16–17 / 18–24 / 25–34 / 35+
- "Would you pay for this?" (optional): No, only free / A few euros per month / €5–10 per month / One-time purchase per skill / Not sure
- Consent checkbox (required): agree to the privacy statement and to receive updates

Rules:
- **Under 16**: do NOT store the email (Dutch GDPR consent age). Show a friendly message: they can try the demo with a parent, or a parent can sign up. Store only an anonymous count of under-16 visits for this form (no personal data).
- Automatically attach: `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content` (captured on first landing and kept in sessionStorage), the referrer domain, the language, the landing path, and the referral code if present.
- Honeypot field plus simple rate limiting per IP (in memory or via the storage provider). Zod validation client- and server-side.
- On success → redirect to `/bedankt`, which shows: "You're on the list", a personal referral link (`?ref=CODE`) with share buttons (WhatsApp, copy link, native share sheet via `navigator.share`), and a one-question follow-up: "What's the one skill you wish someone had taught you?" (free text, optional, linked to the signup).

Storage:
- Supabase (Postgres) with tables `waitlist`, `skill_votes`, `org_leads`, `followups`. Row Level Security: anon can only INSERT, never SELECT. Inserts go through a Next.js route handler using the service role key on the server (preferred), so the client never talks to the database directly.
- Provide the SQL migration file for all tables and policies.
- If the Supabase env vars are missing, fall back to a mock that logs to the server console, so the site runs locally without setup. Show a dev-only badge when in mock mode.
- Optional (behind an env flag): a confirmation email via Resend. Off by default.

## 10. Design direction

- Feeling: warm, human, calm, trustworthy — like a patient older brother/sister, not a tech company. It should feel crafted, not templated.
- Palette: warm off-white background (for example #FAF7F2), deep ink text (for example #1C1B19), one warm accent (for example a deep terracotta or warm green — pick one and use it sparingly), soft neutrals for cards. Dark mode support via `prefers-color-scheme`, with the same warmth.
- Typography: an expressive serif for headlines (for example Fraunces or Newsreader from Google Fonts), a clean humanist sans for body text (for example Inter or Source Sans 3). Large, confident headline sizes on mobile.
- Illustrations: simple, friendly line art (hands, a tie, a razor, an iron, a needle) in ink plus the accent color, drawn as inline SVG. No stock photos, no 3D blobs, no purple gradients, no robot imagery.
- Motion: subtle (fade/slide on scroll, the gentle loop diagram); everything disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Mobile-first: most visitors come from a phone via social media. Design for 375px first; desktop is a nice-to-have enhancement.

## 11. Social media, SEO and analytics

- Dynamic Open Graph images per page and language via `next/og` (headline plus wordmark plus a small illustration), 1200×630, plus Twitter card meta.
- A link-in-bio friendly experience: the first screen loads fast and shows the CTA without scrolling.
- Support `/?ref=CODE` and UTM parameters everywhere; never strip them on internal navigation before they're captured.
- Privacy-friendly, cookieless analytics (Plausible or Umami, configurable via env; disabled if not configured) so no cookie banner is needed. Track events: `cta_waitlist_click`, `cta_demo_click`, `waitlist_submit_success`, `waitlist_under16`, `skill_vote`, `org_form_submit`, `share_click`, `demo_video_play`.
- SEO: proper titles/descriptions in both languages, `hreflang`, sitemap.xml, robots.txt, semantic HTML, and structured data (`Organization` and `FAQPage`).

## 12. Tech stack and quality bar

- Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript (strict), Tailwind CSS, minimal Framer Motion (or CSS only), Zod, Supabase JS (server-side), deployed on Vercel.
- Performance: Lighthouse mobile ≥ 95 for Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO. LCP < 2.0 s on 4G. Self-host or optimize fonts (`next/font`), optimized images, no heavy libraries.
- Accessibility: WCAG AA, keyboard navigable, labeled form fields, visible focus, readable error messages in both languages.
